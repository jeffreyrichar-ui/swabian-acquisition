Your Idaho contractor licence does not transfer with your business. Plumbing,
HVAC and electrical certificates are issued to a person, not a company, and
Idaho law says plainly that they are not transferable. So every sale of a
licensed trade business in the Treasure Valley needs a plan for who holds the
licence on the morning after closing.

That single fact shapes more deals here than anything else I see, and almost
nobody writes about it.

## Which licences are personal, and where it says so

| Trade | Statute | What it says |
|---|---|---|
| Plumbing | Idaho Code 54-2610 | The certificate of competency "shall not be transferable" |
| HVAC | Idaho Code 54-5008 | The certificate of competency "shall not be transferable" |
| Electrical | Idaho Code 54-1003(2) | "No licenses or registrations granted hereunder shall be transferable" |

The company licence is not really a company licence either. Idaho ties it to a
named individual: a plumbing contractor certificate holder may represent one
firm at a time, an electrical contractor must designate a supervising
electrician, and an HVAC entity must employ an active licensed journeyman.
Remove that person from the business and the business cannot legally do the
work, whatever the purchase agreement says.

## So what actually happens at closing?

There are only three real answers, and it is worth knowing which one you are
choosing before a buyer raises it.

- **The buyer holds the licence.** Cleanest, and rare, because it means the
  buyer already carries an Idaho certificate in that trade.
- **A licensed employee becomes the qualifier.** The usual answer. It requires
  that person to stay, which makes their retention agreement part of the deal
  rather than an afterthought.
- **You stay on as the qualifier for a defined period.** Common in a transition,
  and the reason a "clean break at closing" is sometimes not available in a
  licensed trade. It is worth pricing that time, not donating it.

## What else does not travel with the company

Two more that surprise sellers.

A contractor bond does not carry over: HVAC and specialty contractors post a
$2,000 compliance bond that belongs to the licence holder. And Idaho Code
63-3628 makes the buyer of a business responsible for asking the State Tax
Commission what sales and use tax is owed, and for withholding it from the
purchase price until the Commission issues a clearance. A buyer who skips that
step can be held personally liable, which is why a careful buyer will ask for it
and why it is not a sign of mistrust.

## How many businesses does this touch here?

More than most people guess. In 2024 Ada and Canyon counties had 422 plumbing
and heating contractor establishments [fact:F001], 358 electrical contractor
establishments [fact:F002], and 642 landscaping establishments [fact:F003],
counting only employers with payroll. Ada County alone went from 172 plumbing and heating establishments in
2019 to 259 in 2024 [fact:F004], a rise of about half in five years.

Most of those are owned by one person. When that person retires, the licence
retires with them unless somebody planned otherwise.

## What I would do two years out

If you own a licensed trade business in the Treasure Valley and expect to sell
at some point, the single most valuable thing you can do is get somebody other
than yourself licensed to qualify the company. It widens the pool of buyers who
can actually close, it shortens the transition you are obliged to serve, and it
raises what the business is worth to every buyer, not just to me.
